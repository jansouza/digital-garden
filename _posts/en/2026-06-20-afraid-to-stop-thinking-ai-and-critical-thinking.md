I watched a conversation between Leandro Karnal and an AI about God, vanity
and death ([watch it on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5uGP97AmMHY),
in Portuguese), and one line kept hammering in my head:

> "You shouldn't fear that I think. Fear that you stop thinking, because
> it's comfortable to let me do it."

As someone who works with technology every day, it's impossible not to
carry this reflection over to our craft.

## AI is great at writing code. So what?

Nobody argues anymore that AI writes code, suggests architecture and fixes
bugs at impressive speed. That's a done deal. The real danger isn't there.

The danger is in **outsourcing our reasoning**.

I've seen developers who can't explain the code they just submitted. Teams
that stopped questioning architecture decisions because "the AI suggested
it". Nobody asks "why" anymore, they just accept what was generated and
move on.

## The risk isn't technical, it's cognitive

If we stop exercising logic, critical thinking and problem solving — the
fundamental skills behind any good engineering — we risk training a
generation of professionals who know how to operate tools but don't know
how to think about the problem the tool is solving.

And there's a cruel irony in this: if we stop thinking, AI will inherit
exactly the intellectual void we stopped filling. It reflects what we put
into it — shallow questions produce shallow answers, accepted without
question.

## What remains

Using AI isn't the problem. Using AI as a substitute for thinking is.

The question that remains isn't "will AI think for me?", but "am I still
willing to think, even when I have the option not to?".
