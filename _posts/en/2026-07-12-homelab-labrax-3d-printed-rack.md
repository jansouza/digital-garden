When someone asked "what do you do in your free time?", I used to answer
that I compiled the Linux kernel to run on Slackware. These days the answer
is: I have a **homelab**, and it never stops growing 😄

Almost 2 years ago I built my first homelab for home automation. Since
then it has become a serious hobby, and it was time to level up. I'm not
sure whether I bought a 3D printer to print a rack or whether that was just
one more excuse to justify the purchase, but the fact is I used it for
that.

I learned by doing: choosing the filament (I went with PETG, it handles the
heat from the equipment better), print time and orientation, tuning
supports so I don't waste material. Every part coming out of the printer
just right gives you that "it worked" satisfaction.

![LabRax rack next to the 3D printer](/assets/images/homelab-labrax/labrax-rack-impressora-3d.jpeg)

The result was **LabRax**, a project by Michael Klements: a 100% modular,
3D printed rack built to organize Raspberry Pis, mini PCs and network
equipment. With the rack assembled, I expanded my **K3s** cluster with my
Beelink mini PCs. More processing power, more organization, and the setup
finally looks professional.

![LabRax rack seen from the front, with the mini PCs installed](/assets/images/homelab-labrax/labrax-rack-frontal.jpeg)

![Side view of the LabRax rack, with the modular honeycomb panels](/assets/images/homelab-labrax/labrax-rack-lateral.jpeg)

A homelab isn't just having a server at home. It's where I test, break,
fix and learn technologies that I don't always have time to explore in my
day-to-day work. Soon I'll share more technical details about the cluster
architecture and the rack printing process.
