On my [latest LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7479881994135560192/){:target="_blank"},
about how my homelab has evolved, I got a comment asking where to find all
my posts about my maker experiences, gathered in one place. At the time, I
replied that I was keeping everything on LinkedIn itself.

Afterwards I kept thinking about how bad that is: besides the lackluster
organization, finding old posts is almost impossible — not to mention that
it limits access to LinkedIn users only.

I did some quick research and found the "new" (not sure it's that new)
concept of a **digital garden**, a kind of evolution of blogs: a space
where you plant your ideas and, like in a garden, they grow over time.

That's when I decided to create my own Garden. I chose to host it on GitHub
Pages, following the **docs as code** idea — treating documentation as
code, written in plain text and versioned in Git — which I value so much in
my day-to-day work.

And I've already started replanting here: the homelab post that started all
this is at
[Homelab: leveling up with LabRax]({% post_url en/2026-07-12-homelab-labrax-3d-printed-rack %}).

Now you can follow me here:

- [Posts](/en/posts/) — posts in chronological order
- [Tutorials](/tutorials/) — step-by-step guides (in Portuguese)
- [Docs](/docs/) — reference documentation (in Portuguese)

And if you have any suggestions or questions, reach out — after all, it was
a comment that made this garden sprout. Thank you very much!
